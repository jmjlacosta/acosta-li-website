Why it mattered

The model decided who got a call.

At ClosedLoop I was responsible for risk models running across more than 10 million Medicare and Medicaid lives. I think about that number more than anything else I've done, because it was never a dashboard figure. Every one of those was a person with an address and a phone number, and the model helped decide who a care team reached that week.

  • Medicine arriving at someone's door instead of a prescription going unfilled
  • A heart attack that didn't happen, because a care team called before the emergency
  • A ride for a patient with no bus route, so they made the appointment
  • A caregiver assigned to someone who was living alone
  • A blood test someone could finally get at their pharmacy, after years of their veins making a standard draw a problem

That's what I'd want my kids to find if they ever go looking.

Babson Diagnostics · Clinical diagnostics · 2022–2024

Counting platelets that don't want to be counted

For someone whose veins make a standard draw difficult, a blood test is the appointment they skip. Babson's whole point was a draw you could get at a pharmacy, from a finger instead of an arm.

Platelets start clumping at the wound within seconds, and a clumped sample reads as a falsely low count — published studies still find platelet bias beyond allowable limits in roughly a third of capillary samples. Babson's answer was the inverse of Theranos's: stabilise a sample a tenth the size of a normal draw and run it on proven analysers in a real lab. I developed the algorithmic assay that made the platelet count hold up and took it from research prototype through commercial launch, then built the data operations behind that launch at 1,000+ samples a day.

Gates Foundation, via Alithi · Higher education · 2025–

Lucy, a strategy advisor for colleges

College and university leaders mostly know what works: course redesign, proactive advising, credit mobility. What they don't have is a way to tell which of it applies to their institution, at their stage, with their data. Lucy is the multi‑agent system that closes that gap.

It matches an institution against 7,000+ NCES records, works out where that institution sits on a five‑stage transformation journey, and returns only the research that fits, written for the reader's role. Built on Google ADK and Azure OpenAI, delivered as a public good — which is the part that made me want it.

Health Universe × i-Cubed · Clinical trials · 2024–2026

Project LOOM

A documentation system that drafts a trial's entire document set as one coherent body of work: protocol, manual of procedures, safety plan, even patient recruitment materials. Nothing gets invented and nothing drifts. Edit one document and it shows you every place that change contradicts the others. Keeping the documents consistent with each other is the hard problem here. Generating any single one of them is not.

An i‑Cubed Center initiative, built with engineering support from Health Universe's dev team and validated and tested by Duke Clinical Research Institute.
