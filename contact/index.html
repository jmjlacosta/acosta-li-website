What to send

Four things, roughly.

  • The decision the system has to support. Not the technology you want to use — the call somebody has to make, and what they're doing today instead.
  • What data exists. Rough is fine. "It's in three systems and two of them disagree" is a real and useful answer.
  • What's already been tried. A pilot that stalled tells me more about the problem than a clean brief does.
  • The constraint. A regulator, a deadline, a budget, an integration nobody wants to touch. This is usually the thing that decides the design.

What happens next

No pitch deck.

The first conversation is a scoping call. You'll get an honest read on whether the thing is worth building, and if it is, a written scope with a fixed price. If it isn't, I'll tell you that too — it's a shorter conversation and it costs you nothing.

If the fit isn't right but the work is real, I'll point you at someone better suited where I can.

Based in Austin, Texas, working with clients anywhere. Engagements run through Acosta‑Li LLC.
