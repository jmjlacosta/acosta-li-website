How I can help.

A system isn't finished when it works on a laptop. It's finished when it runs on real data, in front of real people, with someone accountable for what it says. Everything below is scoped and priced to get there.

Four practice areas

Most engagements draw on two or three of these at once. A model is only as good as the pipeline feeding it, and an agent is only as useful as the systems it can reach into.

01

Agentic systems & LLM workflows

Multi‑agent pipelines that produce evidence‑backed, auditable output rather than plausible prose. Custom agent frameworks, MCP and agent‑to‑agent architectures, retrieval, fine‑tuning, and evaluation.

Where I usually meet it: Document generation that has to stay consistent across a whole corpus; extraction from messy records; workflows where a wrong answer is worse than none; inference costs that don't survive volume.

02

Machine learning in production

Prediction systems that run every day rather than in a notebook. Training and deployment, drift and bias monitoring, and the integration work that connects a model to the systems people already use.

Where I usually meet it: A model that performs in validation and not in the field; no monitoring, so nobody knows when it degrades; predictions nobody acts on because they arrive where no one is looking.

03

Data operations & infrastructure

The plumbing that decides whether any of it works. EHR and FHIR integration, lab instruments, LIS, CRM, warehouse. Pipelines from prototype to production, with monitoring that holds up under audit.

Where I usually meet it: Data that exists but can't be joined; manual reconciliation nobody has time for; integrations that break silently; reports the business argues with instead of using.

04

Risk, forecasting & analytics

Claims forecasting, financial risk modelling, cohort and outcomes analysis, supply chain vulnerability. Numbers a leadership team can act on and defend.

Where I usually meet it: Forecasts that miss badly enough to be ignored; risk scores without an intervention attached; analysis that can't be explained to the person who has to sign off.

How to work with me

Four ways in.

Scoping sprint

One to two weeks · Fixed price

A working session and a short investigation: the decision the system has to support, the data that actually exists, and what good has to look like. You leave with a written scope, an architecture, and a fixed price for the build, whether or not you do it with me. Most engagements start here, and some usefully end here.

Build engagement

Weeks to quarters · Fixed-price phases

The work itself, in short cycles that each end with something running. You see the real system on your real data instead of a deck describing one. Scope changes get priced before they get built, and it ends with deployment, monitoring, documentation, and your team trained to own it.

Advisory

Ongoing · Retainer or equity

Architecture review, technical due diligence, hiring, and a second opinion on build‑versus‑buy. For teams with engineers who need direction more than hands. Some advisory relationships grow into hands‑on delivery; that's a separate conversation and a separate rate.

Team augmentation

Project basis

When a project needs more than one person, I bring in a bench I've worked with for years: four data scientists and two project managers. You scale without being handed a stranger. For work that needs a full delivery organisation behind it, I partner with Alithi Management Consulting.

How an engagement runs

Three stages. You own it at the end.

Stage one

Scope

Define the decision, audit the data, agree on what good looks like. Ends with a written scope and a fixed price.

Stage two

Build

Short cycles, each ending in something that runs. Real data, not a sandbox. Scope changes priced before they're built.

Stage three

Hand off

Deployed into your stack with monitoring, documentation, and your team trained to run it. The goal is that you don't need me afterward.

Got one of these?

The first conversation is a scoping call, not a pitch. Bring it in whatever state it's in, and you'll get an honest read on whether it's worth building.
